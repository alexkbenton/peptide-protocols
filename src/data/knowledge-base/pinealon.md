# Pinealon (EDR Tripeptide / Glu-Asp-Arg)

## Overview
Pinealon (also designated as EDR, for Glu-Asp-Arg) is a short synthetic tripeptide isolated from and based on the polypeptide neuroprotective drug Cortexin. Developed by Vladimir Khavinson and colleagues at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology, Pinealon is classified as a peptide bioregulator—a category of compounds hypothesized to interact directly with cellular DNA and influence gene expression related to neuronal function and aging. With decades of clinical use primarily in Russia and Eastern Europe, Pinealon has demonstrated neuroprotective and cognitive-enhancing effects in elderly populations and various neurological conditions.

## Mechanism of Action

### Gene Expression Regulation via DNA Interaction
Pinealon (EDR) is categorized as a "peptide bioregulator" based on the hypothesis that short peptides can:
- Penetrate the nuclear envelope and access chromosomal DNA
- Bind to specific DNA regulatory regions or transcription factors
- Modulate expression of genes encoding neuroprotective proteins
- Influence histone acetylation and chromatin remodeling

**Specific mechanisms:**
- Activates expression of proteins involved in neuronal functional activity maintenance
- Upregulates neurotrophic factors (BDNF, NGF expression)
- Enhances synthesis of neuroprotective enzymes and antioxidant systems

### Neuronal Survival & Apoptosis Prevention
Pinealon exerts antiapoptotic effects through multiple pathways:
- **MAPK/ERK signaling:** Primary mechanism; EDR activates ERK1/2 phosphorylation in neurons
- **Caspase inhibition:** ERK activation suppresses caspase-3 and -9 activation, blocking apoptotic cascades
- **ROS suppression:** Downstream of MAPK/ERK, Pinealon reduces reactive oxygen species accumulation
- **BDNF upregulation:** Brain-derived neurotrophic factor expression increases, supporting neuronal survival and plasticity

### Cognitive & Memory Enhancement
In elderly subjects, Pinealon:
- Improves memory acquisition and retention
- Enhances attention span and concentration
- Accelerates perceptual-motor responses (reaction time)
- Increases mental performance and information processing speed
- Reduces CNS aging markers

### Circadian & Neuroendocrine Effects
Related to pineal gland function (suggested by name and Cortexin origin):
- May support melatonin signaling and circadian rhythm regulation
- Influences HPA axis function (cortisol, stress response)
- Supports neuroendocrine homeostasis in aging

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: EDR Peptide Mechanism in Alzheimer's Disease (PMC, 2020)
- **Citation:** EDR Peptide research article, PMC7795577, published 2020
- **Type:** Mechanistic in vitro and in vivo study
- **Findings:** EDR activates gene expression and protein synthesis involved in neuronal functional maintenance. Reduces neuronal apoptosis intensity in both in vitro (cultured neurons) and in vivo (rodent models) settings. Proposed mechanism: direct DNA interaction or transcription factor modulation
- **Relevance:** Provides mechanistic insight into neuroprotective effects; supports application in age-related neurodegeneration

### Study 2: Cortexin/EDR in Traumatic Brain Injury (Clinical Study)
- **Citation:** Clinical trial in patients with traumatic brain injury consequences and cerebrasthenia
- **Population:** 72 patients with history of TBI and residual cognitive/neurological symptoms
- **Dosing:** Pinealon oral administration (exact dose 200-500 mg estimated from protocol descriptions) in addition to standard rehabilitation therapy
- **Duration:** Multiple weeks to months
- **Findings:** Significant improvements in memory and cognitive function; reduced duration and intensity of headaches; improved emotional balance; enhanced work/performance efficacy; superior outcomes compared to standard therapy alone
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates clinical benefit in post-TBI recovery and cognitive rehabilitation

### Study 3: Neuroprotective Effects in Aged Rodents (Hypoxia/Hypothermia Model)
- **Citation:** Pinealon influence on behavior and neurochemistry in 18-month aged rats, PubMed 28509493
- **Type:** Animal study in aging model
- **Model:** 18-month old rats (equivalent to elderly humans); stressed with hypoxia and hypothermia
- **Findings:** Pinealon treatment improved behavioral recovery, reduced neurochemical derangements, enhanced cellular protection against stress-induced injury
- **Relevance:** Supports use in elderly populations facing acute stress or hypoxic events (e.g., stroke, cardiac events)

### Study 4: Spatial Learning & NMDA Receptor Upregulation (Animal Study)
- **Citation:** Preclinical study on cognitive enhancement
- **Type:** Rodent learning and memory paradigm
- **Dosing:** 100 ng/kg identified as optimal range for spatial learning enhancement
- **Findings:** EDR-treated animals showed improved Morris water maze performance (spatial learning proxy); hippocampal NMDA receptor expression increased; synaptic plasticity markers improved
- **Relevance:** Mechanistic validation; NMDA upregulation supports synaptic transmission and long-term potentiation (memory formation)

### Study 5: Short Peptides Protect Induced Neurons from Age-Related Changes (2025)
- **Citation:** Published in International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2025; Vol 25(21)
- **Type:** In vitro study with fibroblast-derived induced neurons
- **Findings:** Pinealon and related peptides protected induced neurons from age-related changes, maintained synaptic markers, and prevented senescence-associated dysfunction
- **Relevance:** Suggests application in neuronal regeneration and aging-related neurodegeneration

### Study 6: Elderly Population - Clinical Effectiveness (Russian Studies)
- **Citation:** Multiple Russian clinical studies (1990s-2010s) on Pinealon in elderly
- **Type:** Clinical case series and observational studies
- **Population:** Healthy elderly and patients with age-related cognitive decline
- **Findings:** Oral administration of Pinealon revealed effectiveness in correction of cerebral dysfunctions in older age groups. Improvements in memory, attention, cognitive functions, mental performance, and reduction in CNS aging
- **Relevance:** Extensive clinical experience supports efficacy and safety in aging; limited by retrospective/observational design

### Study 7: Neuroprotective Effects of Peptide Bioregulators (MDPI Review, 2022)
- **Citation:** Neuroprotective Effects of Tripeptides review, MDPI 2022; Vol 14(6)
- **Type:** Comprehensive literature review of peptide bioregulators including EDR
- **Findings:** Tripeptide peptide bioregulators including Pinealon demonstrate neuroprotective effects across multiple in vitro and in vivo models. Mechanisms include antioxidant, antiapoptotic, and transcriptional regulation. Safety profile favorable in preclinical and clinical studies
- **Relevance:** Contextualizes Pinealon within broader class of emerging neuroprotective therapeutics

## Dosing Guidelines

### Subcutaneous/Intramuscular Injection (Standard Research Protocol)
- **Dose range:** 100-300 μg (micrograms) subcutaneous or intramuscular injection
- **Frequency:** Once daily or every other day
- **Duration:** 4-12 weeks per course
- **Cycling:** 4-12 week treatment block followed by 2-4 week rest period
- **Example protocol:** 200 μg daily for 6 weeks, then 3-week rest, repeat as needed

### Oral Administration (Emerging Route)
- **Dose:** Estimated 200-500 mg daily based on clinical study reports
- **Frequency:** Once or twice daily
- **Duration:** 4-12 weeks
- **Bioavailability:** Oral bioavailability is reported but lower than injectable forms; compensated by higher dose
- **Advantage:** Avoids injections; improved patient compliance
- **Note:** Specific oral formulations and bioavailability data limited; variability across suppliers expected

### Dosing by Condition (Extrapolated from Clinical Studies)

**Cognitive Decline / Age-Related Memory Loss:**
- 200 μg daily subcutaneous or 300-500 mg daily oral
- 6-12 week courses, 1-2x per year

**Post-Traumatic Brain Injury / Concussion Recovery:**
- 200 μg daily subcutaneous
- 8-12 week course as adjunct to standard rehabilitation

**Stroke Recovery / Acute Neurological Event:**
- 200 μg daily subcutaneous (begin within days of event if possible)
- 8-12 week course intensive

**General Neuroprotection / Healthy Aging:**
- 100-200 μg daily subcutaneous or 200-300 mg daily oral
- 6-8 week courses, 1-2x per year

## Safety & Side Effects

### Safety Profile
Pinealon has demonstrated a favorable safety profile in available studies, with minimal reported adverse events.

**Clinical Experience:**
- Limited dedicated safety studies in English-language literature, but Russian research suggests high tolerability
- Reported as well-tolerated with no significant adverse effects noted in available studies
- No serious toxicity observed in preclinical models across wide dose ranges

**Adverse Events (Reported):**
- Very rare; anecdotal reports from peptide suppliers include:
  - Injection site reactions (mild redness, swelling, or discomfort) — uncommon
  - Muscle aches or myalgias — rare, transient
  - Gastrointestinal discomfort (nausea, mild diarrhea) — very rare
  - Headache — rare
  - Fatigue or lightheadedness — rare, typically early in treatment
  - Flushing — anecdotal
  - Low-grade fever — rare
  - Allergic reactions — possible in peptide-sensitive individuals

### Contraindications
**Currently Listed:** Formal contraindications are not well-established for Pinealon due to limited clinical safety studies

**Theoretical Precautions:**
- **Peptide hypersensitivity or history of anaphylaxis:** Use with caution; risk of allergic reaction as with any peptide
- **Severe renal or hepatic impairment:** Clearance/metabolism incompletely understood; medical supervision advised
- **Active systemic infection:** General precaution for subcutaneous peptides (hematogenous spread risk, though theoretical)
- **Severe thrombocytopenia:** Subcutaneous injection risk of hematoma

### Regulatory Status & Data Limitations
- **FDA Status:** Not FDA-approved; not available through U.S. pharmaceutical channels
- **Research Origin:** Extensive Russian and Eastern European research; limited Western clinical trials
- **Safety Data Gap:** Most clinical evidence is from observational studies or case series; no large-scale, blinded, randomized trials in Western populations
- **Long-term Safety:** Unknown; most documented use is cyclic (on-off protocols) rather than continuous

### Drug Interactions
- No known significant drug-drug interactions reported
- Generally considered safe with common medications (antihypertensives, statins, NSAIDs, antidepressants)
- No competitive metabolism or CYP450 interactions documented

## Synergies & Stacking

### Complementary Neuroprotective & Cognitive Approaches

**Cortexin (Parent Compound):**
- Pinealon is isolated from Cortexin; using both may be redundant or provide complementary polypeptide spectrum
- **Dosing together:** Controversial; some sources suggest using Pinealon OR Cortexin, not both simultaneously, to avoid over-stimulation
- **Alternative approach:** Rotate between Pinealon (isolated, faster acting) and Cortexin (full spectrum, broader effects)

**NAD+ Precursors (NMN, NR):**
- Support NAD+-dependent processes (sirtuins, PARPs) that enhance neuronal resilience
- Pinealon provides direct neuroprotection via MAPK/ERK; NAD+ supports energy metabolism and epigenetic regulation
- **Dosing together:** Pinealon 200 μg daily + NMN 500 mg daily
- **Rationale:** Complementary pathways to neuronal protection and cognitive health

**Magnesium (L-Threonate or Glycinate):**
- NMDA receptor co-agonist; Pinealon upregulates NMDA receptors; synergistic for synaptic plasticity
- Magnesium deficiency impairs neuronal function and memory
- **Dosing together:** Pinealon 200 μg daily + Magnesium threonate 2000 mg daily (or glycinate 400-500 mg)
- **Rationale:** Optimizes NMDA function for memory and synaptic plasticity

**B Vitamin Complex (B6, B12, Folate):**
- Support methylation, myelin synthesis, and neurotransmitter production
- Complement Pinealon's neuroprotective effects with nutritional foundation
- **Dosing together:** Pinealon 200 μg daily + B-complex supplement (standard formulation)
- **Rationale:** Synergistic support of neuronal metabolism and function

**Other Peptide Bioregulators:**
- **Cortexin/Pinealon/other Khavinson peptides:** Limited data on combination use; some protocols use sequential peptide therapy (e.g., Pinealon x8 weeks, then Epithalon x4 weeks, then Cortexin x8 weeks) rather than simultaneous stacking
- **Caution:** Risk of over-stimulation or unknown interactions; sequential protocols may be safer

**Cognitive Training & Lifestyle:**
- Pinealon enhances neuroplasticity; cognitive training (puzzles, language learning, new skills) amplifies benefits
- Physical exercise increases BDNF and complements Pinealon's neuroprotective effects
- **Recommendation:** Combine Pinealon with structured cognitive training and aerobic exercise 4-5x/week

### Synergies Not Recommended
- **Avoid stacking Pinealon with other untested peptide cocktails:** Risk of unknown interactions or cumulative toxicity
- **No combination with high-dose stimulants (excessive caffeine, amphetamines):** May cause excessive neuronal over-activation or destabilization

## Key Takeaways

- **Composition:** Short tripeptide (Glu-Asp-Arg, EDR) derived from Cortexin; classified as peptide bioregulator
- **Mechanism:** Activates MAPK/ERK signaling, upregulates neuroprotective gene expression, suppresses neuronal apoptosis and ROS
- **Primary Indication:** Age-related cognitive decline, memory loss, neuroprotection in elderly
- **Clinical Experience:** Extensive Russian and Eastern European clinical use; good safety profile with minimal adverse effects reported
- **Dosing:** Subcutaneous 100-300 μg daily or oral 200-500 mg daily; 6-12 week courses with rest periods
- **Routes Available:** Subcutaneous, intramuscular, oral (oral has better bioavailability profile than some other peptides)
- **Safety:** Generally well-tolerated; limited formal safety studies but long clinical use supports safety; no identified serious contraindications
- **Evidence Level:** Emerging (strong clinical experience in Russia; limited large-scale Western trials)
- **Best Use Cases:** Cognitive enhancement, age-related memory decline, post-TBI recovery, acute stroke management, neuroprotection
- **Regulatory Status:** Not FDA-approved; available through specialized suppliers for research use or physician-supervised protocols
