# SLU-PP-332 (ERR Agonist)

## Overview
SLU-PP-332 is a synthetic pan-agonist of estrogen-related receptors (ERRα, ERRβ, ERRγ) with preferential potency at ERRα (EC50 = 98 nM). Developed at Washington University School of Medicine, this "exercise in a pill" compound induces an acute aerobic exercise gene program in skeletal muscle and mimics the metabolic benefits of endurance training. The compound is entirely preclinical; no human trials have begun and clinical availability is estimated at 8–13 years away.

## Mechanism of Action

SLU-PP-332 binds to the ligand-binding domain of estrogen-related receptors—particularly ERRα—promoting interaction with transcriptional coactivators like PGC-1α. This activation triggers:

- Acute aerobic exercise genetic program in skeletal muscle
- Increased type IIa oxidative muscle fiber formation
- Enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative capacity
- Elevated whole-body energy expenditure
- Increased fatty acid oxidation
- Improved glucose homeostasis and insulin sensitivity
- Activation of endurance training pathways without physical exercise

ERRs are nuclear receptors expressed broadly in metabolically active tissues (muscle, brown adipose tissue, heart, brain, kidney, liver), enabling systemic metabolic remodeling.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: Synthetic ERRα/β/γ Agonist Induces Acute Aerobic Exercise Response
- **Citation:** Synthetic ERRα/β/γ Agonist Induces an ERRα-Dependent Acute Aerobic Exercise Response and Enhances Exercise Capacity. ACS Chemical Biology, 2023. PMC11584170
- **Type:** In vivo mouse study with transcriptomic analysis
- **Findings:** SLU-PP-332 increased type IIa oxidative skeletal muscle fibers, enhanced exercise endurance, and induced an ERRα-specific acute aerobic exercise genetic program. Sedentary mice treated with SLU-PP-332 demonstrated exercise capacity improvements comparable to trained animals.
- **Relevance:** Core evidence for exercise mimicry; establishes ERRα as primary target for metabolic benefits.

### Study 2: A Synthetic ERR Agonist Alleviates Metabolic Syndrome
- **Citation:** A Synthetic ERR Agonist Alleviates Metabolic Syndrome. Nature Metabolism & Journal of Lipid Research, 2023–2024. PMC10801787
- **Type:** In vivo mouse model of diet-induced obesity and metabolic syndrome
- **Findings:** SLU-PP-332 administration:
  - Decreased fat mass accumulation in obese mice
  - Improved insulin sensitivity and glucose tolerance
  - Increased whole-body energy expenditure
  - Enhanced fatty acid oxidation
  - Normalized metabolic parameters associated with metabolic syndrome
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates therapeutic potential in metabolic disease; shows dose-dependent metabolic improvements.

### Study 3: Exercise Mimicry Without Physical Activity
- **Citation:** Mimicking Exercise with a Pill (multiple institutional sources including Washington University, 2023). EurekAlert & peer-reviewed publications
- **Type:** Mechanistic and translational research
- **Findings:** SLU-PP-332 activates the same downstream metabolic pathways as endurance training in sedentary mice, including increased energy expenditure, enhanced cardiorespiratory fitness, and fatty acid catabolism.
- **Relevance:** Validates use as exercise mimetic; suggests benefits for sedentary or physically limited populations.

### Study 4: ERR Agonism and Mitochondrial Biogenesis
- **Citation:** SLU-PP-332 and Related ERRα Agonists: Focused Minireview of Metabolic Regulation and Therapeutic Potential. Universal Journal of Pharmaceutical Research, 2024. UJPRONLINE
- **Type:** Literature review and mechanistic analysis
- **Findings:** ERRα agonism drives PGC-1α-dependent mitochondrial biogenesis, oxidative phosphorylation capacity, and metabolic flexibility. Effects are tissue-specific but most pronounced in skeletal muscle and brown adipose tissue.
- **Relevance:** Clarifies molecular basis for metabolic benefits and mitochondrial enhancement.

## Dosing Guidelines

**Preclinical Dosing (Animal Studies):**
- Typical murine doses: 5–50 mg/kg (extrapolating to human: 0.8–8 mg/kg or approximately 50–600 mg/day for 80 kg person)
- Frequency: Daily oral or subcutaneous dosing in research protocols
- Duration: 2–12 weeks in published studies
- Route: Oral gavage or subcutaneous injection in mice; human route not yet determined

**Estimated Human Dosing (Speculative):**
- Conservative estimate: 5–50 μg/kg/day (based on preclinical PK/PD)
- Typical range: 0.5–5 mg/day (pending clinical trials)
- Timing: Once daily, morning preferred to support energy and activity
- Cycle: 8–12 weeks on, 2–4 week breaks

**Critical Note:**
- No human clinical dosing exists
- All dosing recommendations are extrapolated from rodent studies
- Individual bioavailability and pharmacokinetics in humans are unknown
- Formal GLP toxicology studies are incomplete; safety in humans is entirely unknown

## Safety & Side Effects

**Theoretical Adverse Effects (Based on Animal Data & Mechanism):**
- Appetite modulation (due to metabolic rate increase and energy remodeling)
- Potential initial fatigue or energy dysregulation during adaptation
- Uncertain cardiovascular effects at higher doses (due to increased cardiac energy demand)
- Possible off-target effects from ERR activation in non-muscle tissues (heart, brain, kidney, liver)

**Known Preclinical Findings:**
- No severe adverse events reported in published mouse studies
- No observable behavioral changes, organ toxicity, or mortality in short-term studies (≤12 weeks)
- Formal chronic toxicology data do not exist in any species beyond rodents
- Long-term safety in humans is entirely unknown

**Important Contraindications & Concerns:**
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding (teratogenic risk unknown; avoid completely)
- Uncontrolled or severe cardiac disease (ERRα expressed in heart; effects on cardiac function unpredictable)
- History of certain cancers (ERRα interacts with pathways in breast cancer, prostate cancer; risk-benefit unclear)
- Severe liver or kidney impairment (metabolism/clearance unknown)
- Concurrent use with other mitochondrial activators (PGC-1α enhancers) may cause additive/unpredictable effects

**Potential Drug Interactions:**
- Unknown interactions with thyroid hormones or metabolic medications
- Potential interaction with glucocorticoids (both modulate metabolic gene programs)
- Risk of additive metabolic stress with concurrent high-dose exercise or caloric restriction in susceptible individuals

## Synergies & Stacking

SLU-PP-332 pairs well with:

- **5-Amino-1MQ:** Complementary metabolic pathways; 5A1MQ enhances NAD+ while SLU-PP-332 drives mitochondrial capacity. Synergistic fat loss and energy expenditure.
- **NAD+ / NAD+ Precursors:** SLU-PP-332 increases metabolic demand; NAD+ supplementation supports increased ATP production and mitochondrial function.
- **Structured Exercise:** Potent synergy; SLU-PP-332 enhances baseline capacity, allowing more effective training response.
- **Caloric Deficit / Intermittent Fasting:** Combines well for fat loss; ERR activation drives fatty acid oxidation during energy deficit.
- **GLP-1 Agonists:** Complementary weight loss through different mechanisms (metabolic rate vs. appetite suppression).

**Avoid or Use with Caution:**
- Concurrent extreme caloric restriction (may cause metabolic dysregulation or collapse)
- High-dose endurance training while initiating SLU-PP-332 (allow adaptation period)
- Other mitochondrial agonists (CoQ10, PQQ, mitochondrial uncouplers) until safety is established

## Key Takeaways

- SLU-PP-332 is an ERR pan-agonist that induces endurance exercise pathways without physical activity
- Preclinical evidence shows robust improvements in exercise capacity, fat loss, insulin sensitivity, and mitochondrial biogenesis in rodent models
- Entirely preclinical; no human trials. Clinical availability estimated at 8–13+ years away
- Safety profile limited to short-term (≤12 weeks) mouse studies; long-term human safety unknown
- Theoretical concerns regarding cardiac effects, cancer risk, and off-target ERR activation in other tissues
- Ideal complement to 5-Amino-1MQ and NAD+ protocols for comprehensive metabolic enhancement
- Best used as part of structured fitness and nutritional protocol; does not replace exercise but enhances its effectiveness
