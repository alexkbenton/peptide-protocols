# 5-Amino-1MQ (NNMT Inhibitor)

## Overview
5-Amino-1MQ is a selective small-molecule inhibitor of nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) that enhances cellular NAD+ availability by blocking an enzyme responsible for NAD+ depletion. Preclinical studies demonstrate potent effects on obesity, metabolic syndrome, and insulin sensitivity in rodent models. The compound has not yet entered human clinical trials and is not FDA-approved.

## Mechanism of Action

5-Amino-1MQ works by inhibiting NNMT, the enzyme responsible for converting nicotinamide to 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA)—a reaction that depletes intracellular NAD+ reserves. By blocking this pathway, 5-Amino-1MQ preserves cellular NAD+ pools, leading to:

- Increased intracellular NAD+ levels
- Enhanced mitochondrial function and oxidative capacity
- Suppressed lipogenesis (fat production) in adipocytes
- Increased energy expenditure
- Improved insulin sensitivity and glucose tolerance
- Reduced white adipose tissue (WAT) accumulation

The compound shows high selectivity for NNMT and does not inhibit related SAM-dependent methyltransferases or NAD+ salvage pathway enzymes.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: NNMT Inhibition Reverses High-Fat Diet-Induced Obesity
- **Citation:** Selective and membrane-permeable small molecule inhibitors of nicotinamide N-methyltransferase reverse high fat diet-induced obesity in mice. PMC5826726 (PubMed)
- **Type:** In vitro and in vivo animal study
- **Findings:** 5-Amino-1MQ-treated mice demonstrated significantly reduced body weights, white adipose masses, and adipocyte sizes. Treatment led to 35% reduction in fat mass, 30% decrease in adipocyte size, and 40% decrease in adipocyte volume.
- **Relevance:** Foundational evidence for weight loss and fat mass reduction; establishes dose range and metabolic effects.

### Study 2: NNMT Inhibition Mitigates Obesity-Related Metabolic Dysfunction (2024)
- **Citation:** Nicotinamide N-methyltransferase inhibition mitigates obesity-related metabolic dysfunction. Babula et al., Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism, 2024. PMC11622326
- **Type:** In vivo mouse model study (diet-induced obesity)
- **Findings:** 5-Amino-1MQ treatment dose-dependently limited body weight and fat mass gains, improved oral glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity, and suppressed hyperinsulinemia. Reduced fasting serum insulin by 50-60% in obese mice.
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates glycemic control benefits and dose-response relationship.

### Study 3: NNMT Inhibition with Diet Modification Establishes Distinct Microbiome
- **Citation:** Reduced calorie diet combined with NNMT inhibition establishes a distinct microbiome in DIO mice. Scientific Reports, Nature Publishing Group. s41598-021-03670-5
- **Type:** In vivo mouse study with microbiome analysis
- **Findings:** Combined NNMT inhibition + caloric restriction produced synergistic metabolic improvements through microbiome modulation, suggesting additional mechanisms beyond direct NAD+ enhancement.
- **Relevance:** Indicates potential synergy with lifestyle interventions and gut microbiome benefits.

### Study 4: Roles of NNMT in Obesity and Type 2 Diabetes
- **Citation:** Roles of Nicotinamide N-Methyltransferase in Obesity and Type 2 Diabetes. Liu et al., BioMed Research International, 2021. PMC8337113
- **Type:** Mechanistic review and original research
- **Findings:** NNMT overexpression correlates with obesity severity. NNMT inhibition increases energy expenditure, reduces body weight, improves insulin sensitivity, and normalizes glucose tolerance.
- **Relevance:** Establishes NNMT as a therapeutic target and clarifies physiological role in metabolic disease.

### Study 5: NNMT in Cardiovascular Disease
- **Citation:** Nicotinamide N-Methyltransferase in Cardiovascular Diseases: Metabolic Regulator and Emerging Therapeutic Target. MDPI Biomolecules, 2024
- **Type:** Mechanistic review
- **Findings:** NNMT dysregulation contributes to cardiovascular dysfunction; inhibition may benefit CVD patients through improved NAD+ bioavailability and endothelial function.
- **Relevance:** Suggests cardioprotective mechanisms beyond weight loss.

## Dosing Guidelines

**Oral Administration:**
- Starting dose: 50–100 mg once daily with meals
- Typical maintenance: 100 mg once to twice daily
- Maximum observed: 300 mcg (μg) daily for metabolic enhancement
- Cycle: 4–12 weeks on, with 1-month breaks every 2 months
- Timing: Take earlier in day (morning to early afternoon) to avoid potential sleep interference

**Subcutaneous Injection:**
- Typical range: 50–200 mg per injection
- Frequency: Variable (3x/week to daily protocols reported in research contexts)
- Administration: Rotate injection sites to prevent lipohypertrophy

**Important Notes:**
- No human clinical dosing data available; recommendations based on preclinical studies and emerging clinical experience
- Individual tolerance varies; start low and titrate based on response
- Take with food if GI upset occurs
- Maintain adequate hydration

## Safety & Side Effects

**Common Side Effects (Mild, Usually Transient):**
- Temporary digestive changes
- Mild headaches (often related to hydration/electrolyte status)
- Muscle soreness (associated with increased fat oxidation)
- Nausea
- Fatigue
- Dizziness
- Drowsiness
- Sleep disturbance (if taken late in day)

**Contraindications (Avoid Use):**
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding (insufficient safety data)
- Kidney disease or impaired renal function
- Liver disease or hepatic impairment
- Active cancer treatment
- Eating disorders or restrictive eating patterns
- Uncontrolled cardiovascular disease
- Age <21 years

**Special Precautions:**
- Limited human safety data; currently a research compound not approved by FDA
- Not recommended for individuals with severe organ dysfunction
- Use caution in those with metabolic disorders requiring medication adjustment
- Work with a healthcare provider to ensure purity, correct dosing, and protocol customization

**Drug Interactions:**
- Potential interactions with medications affecting NAD+ metabolism (e.g., certain antiretrovirals, chemotherapy agents)
- May potentiate effects of GLP-1 agonists and other weight-loss medications
- Interaction data in humans is minimal

## Synergies & Stacking

5-Amino-1MQ combines well with:

- **NAD+ Precursors (NMN, NR, NAD+ IV/SubQ):** Synergistic NAD+ pathway enhancement; may reduce NNMT-induced NAD+ depletion while also boosting NAD+ directly
- **GLP-1 Agonists:** Complementary weight loss mechanisms; NNMT inhibition targets metabolic rate, GLP-1s reduce appetite
- **Caloric Restriction / Intermittent Fasting:** Enhanced fat loss through combined metabolic stimulation
- **Exercise/Resistance Training:** NNMT inhibition increases mitochondrial capacity; pairs well with structured training
- **Mitochondrial Agents:** Combination with CoQ10, PQQ, or other mitochondrial enhancers may amplify energy production
- **Avoid Stacking:** With high-dose niacin/nicotinamide (may interfere with NNMT pathway effects)

## Key Takeaways

- 5-Amino-1MQ is a selective NNMT inhibitor that preserves cellular NAD+, driving metabolic rate and fat loss
- Preclinical data shows 30–40% reductions in fat mass and 50–60% improvements in fasting insulin in obese rodent models
- Not yet in human trials; all dosing and safety information is extrapolated from preclinical research and emerging clinical experience
- Most side effects are mild and transient; primarily GI and neurological
- Strong contraindications in pregnancy, kidney/liver disease, and eating disorders
- Best used as part of a comprehensive metabolic protocol (diet, exercise, other peptides/compounds)
- Synergizes well with NAD+ enhancers and GLP-1 agonists for potentiated weight loss and metabolic improvement
