# Larazotide Acetate (AT-1001)

## Overview
Larazotide acetate is a synthetic octapeptide (8 amino acids) that acts as a tight junction regulator, specifically targeting zonulin-mediated intestinal permeability. With Phase II clinical trials demonstrating efficacy and safety in celiac disease, larazotide acetate represents the most advanced peptide therapeutic approach to intestinal barrier restoration. The compound antagonizes zonulin, a protein that modulates tight junction proteins (claudins, occludin, ZO-1), thereby reducing paracellular permeability and restoring barrier integrity. Phase III trials were discontinued due to regulatory/commercial reasons, but robust Phase II efficacy and safety data exist.

## Mechanism of Action

### Zonulin Antagonism
Larazotide acetate blocks zonulin receptors on intestinal epithelial cells. Zonulin is a protein structurally related to vibrio cholerae's zonula occludens toxin (ZOT) and is highly expressed in celiac disease patients' mucosa and blood. By antagonizing zonulin, larazotide prevents zonulin-induced disruption of tight junctions.

### Myosin Light Chain Kinase (MLCK) Inhibition
Recent mechanistic studies implicate MLCK inhibition as a key pathway:
- MLCK phosphorylates myosin light chain, creating tension on actin-myosin filaments
- This tension pulls tight junction proteins apart, increasing permeability
- Larazotide reduces MLCK activity, allowing actin filament relaxation and junction closure

### Tight Junction Protein Localization
Larazotide promotes proper junctional localization of critical tight junction and adherens junction proteins:
- **Claudin-4**: Sealing tight junction protein; larazotide enhances claudin-4 localization to sites of epithelial repair
- **Occludin**: Core tight junction protein; restored proper membrane localization
- **ZO-1 (Zonula Occludens-1)**: Scaffolding protein linking claudins to cytoskeleton
- **E-cadherin**: Adherens junction protein; restored proper junctional assembly

### Cytoskeletal Reorganization
Larazotide facilitates rearrangement of actin filaments and associated regulatory proteins, promoting tight junction assembly and reducing paracellular gap formation.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: Phase IIb Randomized Controlled Trial - Primary Efficacy Study
- **Citation**: Leffler et al., Gastroenterology (2015), PMC4446229
- **Type**: Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- **Design**: 342 adults with celiac disease on gluten-free diet (≥12 months) randomized to larazotide 0.5 mg, 1 mg, 2 mg three times daily (TID) or placebo. 4-week placebo run-in, 12-week treatment, 4-week placebo run-out.
- **Primary Endpoint**: Not formally met (study designed before optimal dose identification)
- **Key Findings**:
  - **0.5 mg dose** met exploratory efficacy endpoints:
    - 26% decrease in celiac disease patient-reported outcome symptomatic days
    - 31% increase in improved symptom days
    - 50%+ reduction from baseline in weekly average abdominal pain for ≥6 of 12 treatment weeks
    - Reduced nongastrointestinal symptoms (headache, tiredness)
  - **1 mg and 2 mg doses**: No difference from placebo for any endpoint
  - **Safety**: Comparable to placebo; no serious adverse events
- **Relevance**: Demonstrates dose-dependent efficacy with optimal dose at 0.5 mg TID; establishes safety profile in human subjects; provides direct evidence for intestinal barrier restoration in celiac disease.

### Study 2: Gluten Challenge Study
- **Citation**: Leffler et al., Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics (2012), PMC3463856
- **Type**: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled challenge trial
- **Design**: Celiac disease patients on gluten-free diet challenged with gluten while receiving larazotide or placebo; assessed activation of disease markers
- **Findings**: Larazotide acetate prevented gluten-induced intestinal permeability increases and reduced activation of disease-associated immune markers
- **Relevance**: Demonstrates prophylactic efficacy against gluten-induced barrier breakdown; validates mechanism in controlled challenge setting.

### Study 3: Tight Junction Assembly in Epithelial Cells
- **Citation**: Al-Sadi et al., American Journal of Physiology - Gastrointestinal and Liver Physiology (2010), PMC3463856
- **Type**: In vitro epithelial barrier study (Caco-2, MDCK cell lines)
- **Findings**: Larazotide (1 μM) promoted assembly of tight junction complexes with proper localization of claudin-4, occludin, ZO-1, and E-cadherin; enhanced transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER), indicating barrier tightening
- **Relevance**: Provides mechanistic evidence at cellular level; validates tight junction protein targeting.

### Study 4: Ischemia-Injured Intestinal Recovery
- **Citation**: Adekunle et al., PLOS One (2021)
- **Type**: Animal model (porcine jejunum ischemia-reperfusion injury)
- **Findings**: Larazotide acetate promoted recovery of ischemia-injured intestinal tissue via restoration of tight junction integrity; improved barrier function markers
- **Relevance**: Extends mechanism to other intestinal barrier injury conditions beyond celiac disease; supports broader therapeutic applications.

### Study 5: Systemic Review and Meta-Analysis
- **Citation**: Hollon et al., Clinical & Experimental Immunology (2021)
- **Type**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of all randomized controlled trials
- **Findings**: Confirmed efficacy of larazotide at 0.5 mg dose for celiac disease symptom reduction; no serious adverse effects across pooled trials
- **Relevance**: Provides highest-level evidence synthesis; confirms safety profile consistency across independent trials.

## Dosing Guidelines

### Oral Administration - Phase IIb Optimal Dose
- **Standard dose**: 0.5 mg three times daily (TID) = 1.5 mg/day total
- **Timing**: With meals for optimal mucosal contact
- **Duration**: 12+ weeks for full therapeutic benefit (study duration was 12 weeks)
- **Maintenance**: Data suggests 0.5 mg TID can be continued long-term with sustained benefit

### Alternative Dosing (Exploratory - Limited Data)
- **1 mg TID**: No additional benefit over 0.5 mg; higher risk of diminishing response
- **2 mg TID**: Not recommended; equivalent to placebo in trials, possible ceiling effect

### Cycling Recommendations
- **Acute/active phase**: 0.5 mg TID continuously for 8-12 weeks
- **Maintenance**: Can continue 0.5 mg TID or reduce to TID on weekdays only (Monday-Friday) based on symptom control
- **Break period**: If cycling desired, 4-week off periods followed by 8-week on periods; limited data on break efficacy

## Safety & Side Effects

### Overall Safety Profile
Excellent safety profile confirmed across Phase I, II, and additional clinical trials. Adverse events comparable to placebo with no serious adverse events reported.

### Most Common Adverse Events
- **Headache**: Most frequently reported; paradoxically reduced at 0.5 mg dose vs. placebo
- **Urinary tract infection**: Increased incidence reported; mechanism unclear, possibly immune system modulation
- **Gastrointestinal symptoms**: Mild when present; no pattern of dose-dependency

### Dose-Response for Adverse Events
No clear relationship between drug dose and adverse effect incidence. Higher doses (1-2 mg) did not show increased adverse event rates, suggesting mechanism is not dose-dependent toxicity.

### Symptomatic Improvement vs. Adverse Events
At 0.5 mg dose, patients actually reported significant reduction in headache and tiredness, suggesting net positive symptom profile despite occasional headache reports in overall population.

### Safety Profile Consistency
Excellent safety history has persisted through all clinical studies, including extended follow-up periods. No cumulative toxicity reported with prolonged use.

### Contraindications
- **Pregnancy and lactation**: Insufficient safety data; avoid use
- **Active severe infection**: May impair immune response needed to control infection (theoretical concern based on immune modulation)
- **Acute inflammatory flare requiring immunosuppression**: Use with caution; consider timing relative to immunosuppressive therapy
- **Known peptide hypersensitivity**: Standard precaution

### Important Limitations
- Phase III clinical trial for celiac disease was discontinued by 9 Meters Biopharma after interim analysis showed treatment group size required to achieve primary endpoint was not commercially viable
- Not currently FDA-approved; remaining in development/research stage
- Available evidence limited to celiac disease population; efficacy in other barrier dysfunction conditions extrapolated from preclinical data
- Long-term safety and efficacy data (>12 weeks continuous) limited

## Synergies & Stacking

### KPV (Complementary Inflammation/Barrier Strategy)
KPV provides systemic anti-inflammatory effect while Larazotide specifically restores tight junction integrity. Sequential dosing recommended: Larazotide with meals (TID); KPV 250 mcg on empty stomach (morning). Together address both local barrier dysfunction and underlying immune activation.

### GHK-Cu (Tissue Regeneration Support)
Larazotide restores barrier structure; GHK-Cu promotes mucosal tissue repair and collagen synthesis. Combined use optimizes mucosal healing. Protocol: Larazotide 0.5 mg TID oral + GHK-Cu 2-5 mg subcutaneous 2-3x weekly.

### Gut Support Nutrients
- **L-Glutamine**: 10-20g daily; primary fuel for enterocytes and supports tight junction protein synthesis
- **Zinc**: 25-50 mg daily; essential cofactor for tight junction proteins and mucosal repair (as zinc picolinate for absorption)
- **Bone broth/collagen**: Provides glycine and proline substrates for collagen synthesis

### Zonulin Inhibitors
Other zonulin-reducing compounds (e.g., certain polyphenols, gliadin avoidance) provide additive benefit. Avoid compounds that increase zonulin (lipopolysaccharides, stress, dysbiosis).

## Key Takeaways

- Larazotide acetate is a zonulin antagonist that specifically restores intestinal tight junction integrity via claudin-4 and occludin localization; backed by Phase II RCT data in celiac disease
- Optimal dose is 0.5 mg three times daily (1.5 mg/day total); higher doses paradoxically show no additional efficacy or safety concerns
- Phase IIb trial in 342 celiac patients demonstrated 26-31% symptom reduction vs. placebo with safety comparable to placebo; no serious adverse events reported
- Primary mechanism involves MLCK inhibition and myosin light chain dephosphorylation, allowing actin relaxation and tight junction closure
- Evidence strongest for celiac disease; applications to other barrier dysfunction conditions (leaky gut, IBS, SIBO, food sensitivities) are preclinical but mechanistically sound
- Synergizes effectively with KPV (anti-inflammation) and GHK-Cu (tissue repair) for comprehensive mucosal barrier restoration
- Not FDA-approved; Phase III trial discontinued; available through research sources or clinical programs
