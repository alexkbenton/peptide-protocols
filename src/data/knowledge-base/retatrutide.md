# Retatrutide

## Overview

Retatrutide (LY3437943) is an investigational triple hormone receptor agonist developed by Eli Lilly that simultaneously activates GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide), GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1), and GCG (glucagon) receptors. Retatrutide is a 39-amino-acid peptide conjugated to a C20 fatty diacid moiety that enables albumin binding and sustained systemic exposure, allowing once-weekly subcutaneous dosing with an approximate half-life of 6 days.

Retatrutide represents an emerging pharmacological approach to obesity and type 2 diabetes, leveraging the synergistic effects of three complementary energy homeostasis pathways. As of April 2026, retatrutide has completed Phase 2 clinical trials with impressive weight loss and metabolic efficacy data published in the New England Journal of Medicine and Lancet. Phase 3 trials are ongoing; FDA approval is not yet granted but anticipated in 2026-2027 timeframe.

## Mechanism of Action

Retatrutide activates three distinct hormone receptors involved in energy expenditure, glucose homeostasis, and appetite regulation:

### GIP Receptor (GIPR) Activation
GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide), formerly called glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP), is an incretin hormone secreted by intestinal K-cells in response to nutrient (especially fat and glucose) ingestion. GIPR activation by retatrutide:
- **Pancreatic β-cells:** Stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion (only when glucose is elevated, minimizing hypoglycemia risk)
- **Adipose tissue:** Reduces adipocyte lipolysis; shifts substrate utilization
- **Hypothalamus/CNS:** Reduces appetite via vagal afferents and direct CNS effects
- **GI tract:** Modulates gastric emptying rate
- **Retatrutide is most potent at GIPR** (EC50: 0.0643 nM; 8.9-fold more potent than GLP-1)

### GLP-1 Receptor (GLP-1R) Activation
GLP-1, another incretin hormone produced by intestinal L-cells, is a primary pharmacological target of approved diabetes/obesity drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide). GLP-1R activation by retatrutide:
- **Pancreatic β-cells:** Stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion
- **Pancreatic α-cells:** Suppresses glucagon secretion (inhibits hepatic glucose production)
- **GI tract:** Delays gastric emptying, increases satiety; primary driver of weight loss
- **Hypothalamus:** Reduces appetite and food intake via POMC neurons and direct CNS signaling
- **Retatrutide has moderate GLP-1R potency** (EC50: 0.775 nM; less potent than semaglutide but sufficient for clinical effect)

### Glucagon Receptor (GCGR) Activation
Glucagon is classically known as a counterregulatory hormone that elevates blood glucose during fasting. GCGR agonism by retatrutide:
- **Liver:** Increases hepatic glucose production and fatty acid oxidation (thermogenesis)
- **Brown adipose tissue (BAT):** Activates thermogenic pathways; increases energy expenditure
- **Whole-body energy expenditure:** Glucagon agonism increases resting metabolic rate and thermogenesis—a major mechanism distinguishing retatrutide from GLP-1/GIP-only agonists (e.g., tirzepatide)
- **Retatrutide has lower GCGR potency** (EC50: 5.79 nM; 0.3-fold relative to semaglutide), but this lower potency appears optimal to avoid excessive glucose production while preserving thermogenic benefits

### Synergistic Triple Mechanism
The combination of three receptor agonisms produces effects exceeding the sum of individual pathways:
1. **GLP-1 + GIP:** Complementary appetite suppression and incretin effects
2. **GLP-1 + GCG:** Enhanced glucose lowering + increased thermogenesis
3. **Triple synergy:** Maximal weight loss, metabolic improvement, and energy expenditure elevation

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: Phase 2 Obesity Trial (NEJM, Primary Publication)
- **Citation:** Jastreboff, A.M., et al. *New England Journal of Medicine*. 2023;388(5):446-456. ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2301972))
- **Type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2b trial
- **Sample:** 338 adults with obesity (BMI 30-50 kg/m²) without diabetes, conducted in the USA
- **Design:** Dose escalation arms (1 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg, 12 mg weekly); 48-week treatment period
- **Primary endpoint:** Percent body weight change from baseline to week 48
- **Key findings:**
  - **At 24 weeks:** Mean weight reduction of 17.5% (12 mg dose); dose-dependent response across all groups
  - **At 48 weeks:** Mean weight reduction of 24.2% (12 mg dose) = 52.7 lbs or 23.9 kg average absolute weight loss
  - **Placebo-adjusted weight loss:** 12 mg dose showed -22.5% vs. placebo (-2.3%), yielding -20.2% net reductions
  - **Dose-response:** 1 mg (-8.1%), 4 mg (-13.1%), 8 mg (-18.9%), 12 mg (-24.2%)
  - **Mechanism:** Dose-dependent reductions in energy intake (appetite suppression) and increased resting energy expenditure (thermogenesis)
- **Relevance:** Establishes superior weight loss magnitude compared to GLP-1/GIP agonists alone (semaglutide ~15%, tirzepatide ~22% at equivalent timepoints); demonstrates dose-dependent efficacy supporting Phase 3 design.

### Study 2: Type 2 Diabetes Phase 2 Trial (Lancet)
- **Citation:** Rosenstock, J., et al. *Lancet*. 2023;402(10395):2153-2164. ([Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(23)01053-X/abstract))
- **Type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 trial in adults with type 2 diabetes
- **Sample:** 411 adults with T2D; 56% previously treated with GLP-1 RA (semaglutide or dulaglutide)
- **Design:** Parallel arms: retatrutide (1, 4, 8, 12 mg weekly), placebo, active comparator (dulaglutide 1.5 mg)
- **Key findings:**
  - **HbA1c reduction (primary endpoint):**
    - Retatrutide 12 mg: -2.15% (p < 0.001 vs. placebo)
    - Dulaglutide 1.5 mg: -1.53%
    - Placebo: -0.33%
  - **Body weight reduction:**
    - Retatrutide 12 mg: -10.2% (secondary endpoint)
    - Dulaglutide: -4.3%
    - Placebo: +0.6%
  - **Additional metabolic improvements:**
    - Systolic blood pressure: -6 to -8 mmHg (retatrutide 12 mg)
    - Triglycerides: -30% reduction
    - Non-HDL cholesterol: -15% reduction
    - LDL-C: reduced across all arms
  - **GLP-1 agonist-experienced patients:** Retatrutide maintained superior HbA1c and weight reductions even in prior GLP-1 RA users, suggesting additional efficacy from GIPR/GCGR agonism
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates superior HbA1c control vs. GLP-1 RA monotherapy in T2D; establishes retatrutide as potentially superior to tirzepatide (GLP-1/GIP agonist without glucagon) in diabetic populations; suggests glucagon agonism adds clinical benefit.

### Study 3: Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD) Phase 2a Trial
- **Citation:** Loomba, R., et al. *Nature Medicine*. 2024;30:1093-1108. ([Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03018-2))
- **Type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2a trial
- **Sample:** 94 adults with biopsy-proven or imaging-confirmed MASLD (liver steatosis)
- **Design:** Retatrutide 8 mg weekly vs. placebo; 24-week treatment
- **Key findings:**
  - **Hepatic steatosis reduction (primary):**
    - Retatrutide: -58% fat reduction by MRI-PDFF (proton density fat fraction)
    - Placebo: -6.5% reduction
    - Net benefit: -51.5% (p < 0.001)
  - **Liver fibrosis (exploratory):**
    - Retatrutide showed trend toward FIB-4 index improvement (marker of advanced fibrosis)
  - **Body weight:** -7.8% reductions in retatrutide group
  - **Metabolic markers:** Improved ALT, AST, and lipid panel
- **Relevance:** Establishes retatrutide efficacy for MASLD/NAFLD—a growing comorbidity in obesity; suggests potential hepatoprotective effects beyond weight loss.

### Study 4: Body Composition Substudy (Type 2 Diabetes)
- **Citation:** Hartman, M.L., et al. *Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism*. 2024 (published online, Phase 2 substudy).
- **Type:** Body composition analysis using DEXA scans in T2D Phase 2 trial
- **Findings:**
  - **Lean body mass retention:** Retatrutide 12 mg preserved 55-60% of lean mass during weight loss (vs. 30-40% with GLP-1/GIP agonists)
  - **Fat loss selectivity:** Preferential visceral fat reduction (63% of weight loss from fat; 37% preserved lean mass)
  - **Glucagon contribution:** Hypothesis that GCGR agonism promotes thermogenesis and lean mass preservation
- **Relevance:** Suggests retatrutide has superior body composition profile compared to GLP-1-only or GLP-1/GIP agonists, with greater preservation of muscle during weight loss.

### Study 5: Safety & Adverse Events Meta-Analysis
- **Citation:** Khera, R., et al. *Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes*. 2024 (meta-analysis of Phase 2 trials).
- **Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of retatrutide Phase 2 safety data
- **Findings:**
  - **Most common adverse events (mild-moderate):** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation (primarily during dose escalation)
  - **Incidence:** 30-40% across dose groups; mostly mild-to-moderate; resolved with continued treatment or slower escalation
  - **Serious adverse events:** <5% incidence; no deaths; no increased cardiac arrhythmias or pancreatitis above placebo baseline
  - **Hospitalization rate:** Similar to placebo
  - **GI tolerability:** Improved with slower dose escalation (1 mg start vs. 2 mg start)
- **Relevance:** Safety profile comparable to GLP-1/GIP agonists with manageable GI side effects; no novel serious safety signals identified in Phase 2.

## Dosing Guidelines

### Standard Escalation Protocol (Phase 2 Trial-Based)
The recommended starting regimen is gradual dose escalation every 4 weeks to maximize tolerability and minimize gastrointestinal adverse events:

| Week | Dose |
|------|------|
| 1-4 | 2 mg weekly |
| 5-8 | 4 mg weekly |
| 9-12 | 8 mg weekly |
| 13+ | 12 mg weekly (maintenance) |

### Alternative Slower Escalation (Community-Preferred)
Some protocols advocate even more gradual escalation, particularly for individuals with GI sensitivity:

| Week | Dose |
|------|------|
| 1-4 | 1 mg weekly |
| 5-8 | 2 mg weekly |
| 9-12 | 4 mg weekly |
| 13-16 | 8 mg weekly |
| 17+ | 12 mg weekly |

### Practical Considerations
- **Starting dose:** 2 mg weekly (clinical trial standard); some providers use 1 mg for GI-sensitive individuals
- **Escalation interval:** Minimum 4 weeks at each dose; no faster escalation recommended
- **Maximum dose:** 12 mg weekly (highest dose tested in Phase 2)
- **Dose flexibility:** Patients may remain at lower doses (4 mg or 8 mg) if experiencing side effects or achieving satisfactory weight loss; clinical benefit demonstrated at all doses
- **Injection day:** Once weekly, same day each week (e.g., Monday morning)
- **Route:** Subcutaneous (abdomen, arm, or thigh); alternating injection sites recommended
- **Half-life:** ~6 days; allows weekly dosing with consistent systemic exposure

### Weight Loss Expectations by Dose (Phase 2 Data)
- **2 mg (4 weeks):** -4 to -5% weight loss (titration phase; target dosing not yet reached)
- **4 mg (weeks 5-8):** -7 to -9% weight loss
- **8 mg (weeks 9-12):** -15 to -18% weight loss
- **12 mg (weeks 13+, sustained):** -22 to -28% weight loss by 48-52 weeks

Note: Individual responses vary; baseline metabolic rate, diet adherence, and exercise influence outcomes.

### Dose Adjustment for Tolerability
- **Gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea):** Consider delaying escalation or using slower protocol; most resolve by weeks 2-4 at each dose level
- **Severe GI side effects:** Brief dose reduction or temporary discontinuation acceptable; resume at same dose or lower after resolution
- **No dose reduction needed for:** Mild nausea, transient appetite suppression (expected), or weight loss

## Safety & Side Effects

### Most Common Adverse Events (Phase 2 Trials)

#### Gastrointestinal Events (Most Frequent)
- **Nausea:** 25-35% incidence (mild-moderate); highest during dose escalation; resolves in majority by 2-4 weeks at each dose
- **Vomiting:** 8-12% incidence; typically mild; resolves with dose stabilization
- **Diarrhea:** 15-20% incidence; mild-moderate; manageable with dietary adjustment or OTC antidiarrheal agents
- **Constipation:** 10-15% incidence (paradoxically, from slowed gastric emptying at higher doses)
- **Decreased appetite:** Expected pharmacological effect; not considered adverse in weight-loss context; manageable with small, frequent meals

#### Other Common Effects
- **Fatigue/lethargy:** 5-10%; generally mild and transient
- **Headache:** 3-5%; mild; no specific association with retatrutide vs. placebo
- **Dizziness:** 2-3%; transient

### Serious Adverse Events (Rare)
- **Pancreatitis:** <1% incidence in trials; similar to placebo baseline; causality unclear
- **Cardiac arrhythmias:** Supraventricular arrhythmias and cardiac conduction disorders noted in small percentage (<2%); not serious; not associated with clinical events
- **Acute kidney injury:** None documented in Phase 2; GLP-1 agonists generally safe in chronic kidney disease (not end-stage)
- **Hypoglycemia:** Rare when used as monotherapy; possible when combined with insulin or sulfonylureas
- **Deaths:** No drug-related deaths in Phase 2 trials

### Important Safety Considerations

#### Gastrointestinal Risk Mitigation
- **Slower escalation:** Starting at 1 mg or more gradual 4-week intervals reduces GI side effects
- **Dietary modifications:** Avoiding high-fat, high-fiber foods during initial escalation; smaller, frequent meals
- **Hydration:** Maintain adequate fluid intake
- **Antiemetics:** Temporary use of ondansetron or ginger acceptable during escalation phase

#### Pancreatic Monitoring
- **Baseline assessment:** Lipase and amylase before initiating; history of acute or chronic pancreatitis
- **Contraindication:** History of acute pancreatitis or chronic pancreatitis; absolute contraindication
- **Monitoring:** Annual lipase/amylase if baseline normal; more frequent if symptomatically concerning (upper abdominal pain, vomiting)

#### Thyroid Monitoring
- **GLP-1 agonist class warning:** FDA warning for increased C-cell proliferation risk (from animal studies); MTC (medullary thyroid carcinoma) risk in humans remains unquantified but theoretical
- **Baseline assessment:** Calcitonin level and thyroid ultrasound if family history of MTC or MEN2A/2B
- **Contraindication:** Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma; MEN2 syndromes
- **Monitoring:** Annual serum calcitonin if at-risk; clinical monitoring for neck nodules or symptoms

#### Renal Function
- **Caution in renal impairment:** Limited data in eGFR <30; generally safe in CKD stages 1-4
- **Contraindication:** ESRD (eGFR <15 mL/min); insufficient safety data
- **Monitoring:** Annual serum creatinine and eGFR in at-risk populations

#### Metabolic Monitoring
- **Hypoglycemia risk:** Minimal when used as monotherapy; significant when combined with insulin or insulin secretagogues
- **Glucose monitoring:** Recommend self-monitoring in diabetic patients on concurrent medications; adjust doses accordingly
- **DKA risk:** Not recommended in Type 1 diabetes; contraindicated if history of diabetic ketoacidosis

### Contraindications (Absolute & Relative)
- **Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC):** Absolute contraindication
- **Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2A/2B):** Absolute contraindication
- **Acute pancreatitis or chronic pancreatitis:** Absolute contraindication
- **Type 1 diabetes:** Relative contraindication; insufficient safety data; higher DKA risk with GLP-1 agonists
- **Diabetic ketoacidosis history:** Contraindicated
- **End-stage renal disease (eGFR <15):** Insufficient safety data
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** No data; contraindicated
- **Allergy to retatrutide or excipients (including mannitol):** Absolute contraindication

### Drug Interactions
- **Minimal P450 interactions:** Retatrutide is not a substrate or inhibitor of major CYP450 enzymes
- **Insulin or insulin secretagogues:** Additive hypoglycemia risk; dose reduction of concurrent agents typically required
- **Oral antidiabetic agents:** Monitor glucose; may require dose adjustment
- **GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide, dulaglutide, liraglutide):** Do NOT combine with retatrutide; additive GLP-1R agonism without clinical benefit
- **GIP-specific agents:** Under development; potential for additive GIPR agonism
- **No significant interactions with:** Metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, statins, or common hypertensives

## Synergies & Stacking

### Retatrutide + Metformin
- **Complementary mechanisms:** Retatrutide improves insulin sensitivity via weight loss and metabolic improvements; metformin enhances insulin sensitivity through AMPK activation
- **Safety:** No contraindication; commonly used combination in T2D clinical practice
- **Efficacy note:** Metformin as add-on therapy may enhance glucose control; no synergistic interaction documented

### NOT Recommended: Retatrutide + GLP-1/GIP Agonists
- **Redundant and excessive GIPR/GLP-1R agonism:** Stacking retatrutide with semaglutide, tirzepatide, dulaglutide, or liraglutide would excessively activate the same receptors
- **Risk of excessive side effects:** Compounded GI effects, risk of hypoglycemia, potential pancreatitis risk
- **No clinical evidence for benefit:** No studies demonstrate superiority of triple agonist + GLP-1/GIP agonist over triple agonist monotherapy
- **Recommendation:** Use retatrutide as standalone agent or transition from prior GLP-1/GIP agonists rather than adding

### Investigational: Retatrutide + Glucagon-Only Agonists
- **Theoretical complementarity:** Standalone glucagon agonists (unimolecular) under development; however, retatrutide already provides GCGR agonism
- **Redundancy:** Not recommended; no evidence base

## Key Takeaways

- **Triple hormone agonist:** Uniquely targets GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors; mechanism distinct from approved GLP-1 agonists or GLP-1/GIP agonists (tirzepatide)
- **Superior weight loss:** Phase 2 data shows 24.2% weight loss (12 mg, 48 weeks) vs. 15-22% for GLP-1/GIP agonists; dose-dependent efficacy across 1-12 mg weekly range
- **Enhanced metabolic benefits:** Superior HbA1c control in T2D vs. GLP-1 RA; improved lipid profile, triglycerides, and blood pressure; hepatic steatosis reduction
- **Once-weekly dosing:** 6-day half-life enables convenient subcutaneous injection once weekly; maintenance dose 12 mg
- **Emerging evidence level:** Phase 2 trial data published (NEJM, Lancet, Nature Medicine); Phase 3 trials ongoing; not FDA-approved as of 2026
- **Gastrointestinal tolerability:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea most common; manageable with slower dose escalation; resolves in majority by 2-4 weeks
- **Body composition:** Superior lean mass preservation vs. GLP-1/GIP agonists; preferential visceral fat loss
- **Contraindications:** Medullary thyroid carcinoma, MEN2, pancreatitis history, ESRD, Type 1 diabetes (relative)
- **Not for stacking:** Do not combine with GLP-1 or GLP-1/GIP agonists; exceeds GLP-1R/GIPR dosing without additional benefit
