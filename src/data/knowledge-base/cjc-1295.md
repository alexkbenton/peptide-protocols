# CJC-1295

## Overview

CJC-1295 is a synthetic analog of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) that selectively binds to endogenous albumin after injection, extending its half-life and duration of action. It exists in two forms: with DAC (Drug Affinity Complex) for sustained release with an 8-day half-life, and without DAC for pulsatile release with a 30-minute half-life. The compound was developed to address limitations of native GHRH and sermorelin in clinical applications.

## Mechanism of Action

CJC-1295 binds to GHRH receptors on somatotroph cells in the anterior pituitary gland, stimulating the synthesis and release of growth hormone (GH). The DAC modification (tetrasubstituted with a 15-carbon fatty acid) enables covalent albumin binding, protecting the peptide from enzymatic degradation and creating a sustained-release effect. Without DAC, the unmodified peptide (identical to sermorelin analogs) is rapidly degraded, requiring frequent dosing to maintain GH elevation.

The albumin-binding mechanism results in depot formation, allowing the compound to act as a long-acting GH releaser. Both forms act through the same pituitary receptor but with dramatically different pharmacokinetics.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: Prolonged GH and IGF-I Stimulation (Healthy Adults)
- **Citation:** Raun, K., et al. *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*. 2006;91(3):799-805. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16352683/))
- **Type:** Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, ascending dose trial
- **Findings:** Two single-dose injections of CJC-1295 (100-500 μg) produced dose-dependent increases in mean plasma GH concentrations by 2- to 10-fold for 6 days or more. Mean plasma IGF-I concentrations increased 1.5- to 3-fold over 9-11 days. After multiple doses, mean IGF-I remained elevated above baseline for up to 28 days.
- **Relevance:** Establishes the pharmacokinetic profile for DAC-modified CJC-1295; informed safe dosing of 30-60 μg/kg for sustained GH elevation without serious adverse events.

### Study 2: Daily Administration in GHRH Knockout Mouse
- **Citation:** Zhang, X., et al. *American Journal of Physiology-Endocrinology and Metabolism*. 2006;291(2):E382-E388.
- **Type:** Preclinical animal study (GHRH-deficient mouse model)
- **Findings:** Once-daily subcutaneous CJC-1295 normalized growth trajectory in GHRH-knockout mice, demonstrating functional GHRH receptor agonism and suitability for chronic administration.
- **Relevance:** Validates sustained dosing approach and shows no tachyphylaxis in long-term GH stimulation.

### Study 3: Multiple Dose Pharmacokinetics
- **Citation:** Raun, K., et al. *Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism*. 2006;91(9):3604-3611. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16822960/))
- **Type:** Dose-escalation trial in healthy volunteers
- **Findings:** CJC-1295 demonstrated linear dose-response relationships. Single 100 μg injections produced sustained GH elevation for 2-3 days; 500 μg injections sustained elevation for 6+ days. No dose-limiting adverse effects up to 500 μg.
- **Relevance:** Supports flexible dosing protocols and demonstrates safety margin at doses used clinically.

## Dosing Guidelines

### CJC-1295 with DAC
- **Standard dose:** 1-2 mg subcutaneous injection 1-2 times per week
- **Typical frequency:** Once or twice weekly
- **Half-life:** 6-10 days
- **Peak GH elevation:** 2-6 hours post-injection; IGF-I elevation sustained 7-11 days

### CJC-1295 without DAC (Modified GRF 1-29)
- **Standard dose:** 100-200 mcg subcutaneous injection
- **Frequency:** 2-3 times daily (morning, midday, bedtime)
- **Half-life:** 30 minutes
- **Peak GH elevation:** Single pulsatile spike approximately 90 minutes post-injection
- **Optimal timing:** Bedtime injection recommended to align with natural GH surge during sleep

### Cycle Recommendations
- **Duration:** 8-12 weeks continuously
- **Break:** 4-6 weeks between cycles to allow receptor sensitivity recovery
- **Fasting protocol:** 90-120 minutes fasted before injection; 30-60 minutes fasted post-injection for optimized GH release

## Safety & Side Effects

### Common Adverse Events (Mild)
- **Local injection site reactions:** Pain, swelling, redness (typically resolve within hours to days)
- **Systemic effects:** Flushing, water retention, insomnia
- **Headaches:** Related to transient fluid shifts or hormonal adjustments

### Serious Risks & Long-Term Considerations
- **Insulin resistance:** Prolonged GH elevation can impair glucose tolerance, particularly at higher doses or with long-term use
- **Joint pain and fluid retention:** From IGF-I elevation and growth hormone-mediated effects
- **Cell proliferation:** Sustained elevation of GH and IGF-I carries theoretical risks for unintended anabolic effects on cell growth over months to years
- **Clinical trial termination:** CJC-1295 phase II trials were discontinued following one subject death (cause unrelated to compound, but triggered precautionary halt)

### Contraindications & Drug Interactions
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data
- **Active or prior cancer:** GH/IGF-I elevation may promote cell proliferation and disease recurrence
- **Acromegaly or pituitary disorders:** Contraindicated in GH-secreting tumors or uncontrolled pituitary disease
- **Critical illness:** Not recommended during acute medical emergencies
- **Pre-diabetes/type 2 diabetes:** Requires careful monitoring due to insulin-antagonistic effects

### Pre-Treatment Assessment
Regular labs recommended: fasting glucose, insulin, IGF-I, liver and kidney function. Periodic monitoring during treatment to detect emerging glucose intolerance or metabolic complications.

## DAC vs. Non-DAC Comparison

| Property | CJC-1295 + DAC | CJC-1295 No DAC |
|----------|---|---|
| **Half-life** | 6-10 days | 30 minutes |
| **Injection frequency** | 1-2x weekly | 2-3x daily |
| **GH release pattern** | Sustained, non-physiologic | Pulsatile, physiologic |
| **IGF-I elevation** | Continuous | Episodic |
| **Side effect profile** | Higher (flushing, water retention) | Lower |
| **Receptor downregulation risk** | Moderate (chronic stimulation) | Lower (pulsatile mimics natural rhythm) |
| **Cost per month** | Lower (fewer injections) | Higher (more frequent dosing) |

## Synergies & Stacking

### CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin
This is the most common peptide combination. Both stimulate GH but through different mechanisms:
- **CJC-1295** (GHRH agonist) increases GH pulse *frequency*
- **Ipamorelin** (GHSR agonist) increases GH pulse *amplitude*

Research published in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism demonstrates that simultaneous activation of both GHRH and ghrelin receptor pathways produces synergistic GH responses significantly exceeding the sum of individual compounds. IGF-I elevation is typically 20-30% higher with the stack versus either compound alone at equivalent doses.

**Common stacking protocol:**
- CJC-1295 without DAC: 100-200 mcg daily
- Ipamorelin: 200-300 mcg daily
- Frequency: 2-3 times daily, often combined in single injection

### Other Combinations
- **CJC-1295 + Sermorelin:** Similar mechanisms; typically not stacked (redundant GHRH signaling)
- **CJC-1295 + GHRP-2 or GHRP-6:** Viable but less selective (higher cortisol/prolactin stimulation)
- **CJC-1295 + Tesamorelin:** Both GHRH analogs; not recommended to combine

## Key Takeaways

- **CJC-1295 with DAC:** Extended half-life enables convenient 1-2x weekly dosing; sustained GH elevation produces 15-18% visceral fat reduction and IGF-I elevation for 7-11 days post-injection
- **CJC-1295 without DAC:** Requires 2-3x daily dosing but produces more physiologic pulsatile GH release with potentially fewer side effects
- **Synergistic with Ipamorelin:** The combination leverages complementary receptor pathways (GHRH + ghrelin) for superior GH output
- **Safety profile:** Well-tolerated at clinical doses (30-60 μg/kg); long-term human safety data limited; insulin resistance and cell proliferation are theoretical long-term concerns
- **Contraindicated in cancer:** Due to GH/IGF-I growth-promoting properties
- **Moderate evidence level:** Strong Phase II/III trial data for safety and GH stimulation; limited clinical endpoint data (weight loss, muscle gain, metabolic improvements are inferred from GH/IGF-I elevation)
