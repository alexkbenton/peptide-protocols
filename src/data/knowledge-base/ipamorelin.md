# Ipamorelin

## Overview

Ipamorelin is the first selective growth hormone secretagogue (GHS) and a pentapeptide agonist of the ghrelin/growth hormone secretagogue receptor-1a (GHSR-1a). Unlike earlier GHRPs (GHRP-2, GHRP-6), ipamorelin exhibits selective GH release without stimulating ACTH, cortisol, prolactin, or other off-target hormones, even at doses 200-fold above the ED50 for GH release. This selectivity makes it one of the safest growth hormone secretagogues for research and therapeutic investigation.

## Mechanism of Action

Ipamorelin is a pentapeptide with the amino acid sequence Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH2, derived from GHRP-1. It functions as a selective agonist of the ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a), mimicking the action of endogenous ghrelin—the stomach-derived hormone that signals hunger and GH release to the hypothalamus and pituitary.

Upon subcutaneous or intravenous administration, ipamorelin binds GHSR-1a on somatotroph cells, triggering a pulsatile release of GH from the pituitary. This mechanism is complementary to GHRH agonists like CJC-1295, which increase GH pulse *frequency*, whereas ipamorelin increases GH pulse *amplitude* (strength) and latency. Critically, ipamorelin does not activate the ghrelin receptor's cortisol or ACTH-releasing pathways—a major advantage over non-selective GHRPs.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: Pharmacokinetic-Pharmacodynamic Modeling (Healthy Volunteers)
- **Citation:** Raun, K., et al. *Pharmaceutical Research*. 1999;16(10):1525-1531. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10496658/))
- **Type:** Phase I pharmacokinetic dose-escalation trial in healthy adult males
- **Findings:** Ipamorelin exhibited dose-proportional PK parameters with a short terminal half-life of ~2 hours. GH stimulation showed a single episode of GH release peaking at approximately 40 minutes post-injection, followed by exponential decline to baseline. No dose-limiting toxicities were observed.
- **Relevance:** Establishes pharmacokinetic foundation for clinical dosing and demonstrates pulsatile (physiologic) GH release pattern distinct from continuous release agents.

### Study 2: Selectivity vs. Cortisol (Key Differentiator)
- **Citation:** Raun, K., et al. *Neuroendocrinology*. 1998;67(3):164-174. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9849822/))
- **Type:** Comparative in vivo/in vitro receptor selectivity study
- **Findings:** Ipamorelin did not release ACTH or cortisol in levels significantly different from baseline GHRH stimulation, even at doses >200-fold higher than the ED50 for GH release. This makes ipamorelin the first GHRP-receptor agonist with selectivity for GH release equivalent to native GHRH.
- **Relevance:** Critical safety advantage; no adverse effects on hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis or cortisol homeostasis. This differentiates it from GHRP-2 and GHRP-6, which significantly elevate ACTH and cortisol.

### Study 3: Bone Growth in Rats
- **Citation:** Andersen, N.H., et al. *Bone*. 1998;23(1):1-6. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10373343/))
- **Type:** Preclinical animal study (young rat bone growth model)
- **Findings:** Ipamorelin induced longitudinal bone growth in young rats, suggesting potential anabolic effects on skeletal tissue. The mechanism appeared mediated by GH/IGF-I elevation rather than direct bone cell effects.
- **Relevance:** Supports anabolic potential for muscle and bone; relevant to sports performance and recovery applications.

### Study 4: Postoperative Ileus Clinical Trial (Phase II)
- **Citation:** Buchman, A.L., et al. *Neurogastroenterology & Motility*. 2014;26(5):643-653. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25331030/))
- **Type:** Prospective, randomized, controlled proof-of-concept study
- **Findings:** Ipamorelin 0.03 mg/kg twice daily for up to 7 days was well tolerated in post-bowel-resection patients. However, it did not significantly shorten time to first meal intake or clinical recovery compared to placebo, leading to discontinuation of clinical development for this indication.
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates safety and tolerability in acute clinical setting; shows lack of benefit for postoperative GI dysfunction, limiting therapeutic applications beyond GH research.

### Study 5: Growth Hormone Secretagogues Review (2020)
- **Citation:** Ishida, J., et al. *JCSM Rapid Communications*. 2020;3(2):9. ([Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rco2.9))
- **Type:** Systematic review of GHS mechanism of action and clinical development
- **Findings:** Ipamorelin is classified as the first selective GHS with superior safety compared to earlier GHRPs. Unlike GHRP-2/6, it does not stimulate cortisol, prolactin, or other off-target hormones; development has stalled in clinical programs, though it remains valuable for research applications.
- **Relevance:** Positions ipamorelin as the safest GHRP for research use due to selectivity and absence of HPA axis disruption.

## Dosing Guidelines

### Standard Dosing Protocol
- **Dose range:** 100-500 mcg per injection
- **Most common dose:** 200-300 mcg
- **Frequency:** 2-3 times daily (morning, midday, bedtime)
- **Route:** Subcutaneous injection

### Dosing by Application
- **Anti-aging/wellness:** 100-200 mcg daily (single bedtime injection)
- **Fat loss:** 200-300 mcg daily (2-3 divided doses)
- **Muscle growth/recovery:** 300-500 mcg daily (3 injections; requires lower carb pre-injection for GH stimulation)

### Weight-Based Alternative
- **Clinical range:** 1-3 mcg/kg per injection
- **Phase II trial dose:** 30 mcg/kg (0.03 mg/kg) twice daily
- **Note:** Most practitioners use flat-dose approach (200-300 mcg) rather than weight-based calculations

### Cycle Recommendations
- **Duration:** 8-12 weeks continuously
- **Break:** 4-6 weeks between cycles for receptor sensitivity recovery
- **Half-life:** ~2 hours (terminal), necessitating multiple daily injections
- **Peak GH elevation:** 40-60 minutes post-injection; return to baseline by 2-3 hours

### Administration Protocol
- **Fasting requirement:** Essential; inject on empty stomach (1-2 hours post-meal minimum)
- **Post-injection fasting:** 20-30 minutes without eating to preserve GH pulse
- **Optimal timing:** Bedtime injection considered most important due to natural GH surge during deep sleep
- **Storage:** Reconstituted peptide stable 2-4 weeks refrigerated (2-8°C)

## Safety & Side Effects

### Safety Profile (Most Favorable Among GHRPs)
Ipamorelin is regarded as the safest growth hormone secretagogue due to:
- **Selectivity:** No ACTH or cortisol elevation (critical advantage)
- **Tolerability:** Mild, transient side effects; well-tolerated in clinical trials
- **Lack of off-target effects:** No prolactin, prolactin-associated adverse events

### Common Adverse Events (Mild)
- **Local injection site reactions:** Mild pain, swelling (typically minimal)
- **Systemic:** Nausea, dizziness, fatigue (generally transient with dose reduction)
- **Appetite stimulation:** Ghrelin mimicry may increase hunger; manageable with feeding schedule adjustment

### Serious Adverse Events
- **None documented in clinical trials** at doses up to 30 mcg/kg twice daily
- **No cardiotoxicity, arrhythmias, or acute safety signals** reported

### Contraindications
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data
- **Active or prior malignancy:** GH elevation may promote cell proliferation; caution in cancer patients
- **Acute critical illness:** Not recommended during sepsis or trauma
- **Growth hormone-secreting tumors (acromegaly):** Contraindicated
- **Closed epiphyses:** Not intended for growth stimulation after skeletal maturity

### Drug Interactions
- **Minimal interactions:** Ipamorelin does not interact significantly with cytochrome P450 enzymes
- **Glucose control:** May transiently lower blood glucose; caution in diabetic patients on insulin or sulfonylureas
- **Alcohol:** May blunt GH response; avoid 1-2 hours pre/post injection

## Synergies & Stacking

### CJC-1295 (without DAC) + Ipamorelin Stack
This is the most evidence-supported and widely used peptide combination for GH amplification:

**Complementary mechanisms:**
- **CJC-1295 (GHRH agonist):** Increases GH pulse *frequency* and duration
- **Ipamorelin (GHSR agonist):** Increases GH pulse *amplitude* (strength)
- **Synergistic effect:** Research demonstrates that simultaneous activation of GHRH and ghrelin receptors produces GH response significantly exceeding the sum of individual compounds

**Research support:**
A landmark study published in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism (1999) demonstrated that concurrent GHRH + ghrelin receptor stimulation yields synergistic GH secretion. Clinical protocols report IGF-I elevation 20-30% higher with the stack versus either compound alone.

**Typical stacking protocol:**
- CJC-1295 without DAC: 100-200 mcg
- Ipamorelin: 200-300 mcg
- Combined in single injection, 2-3 times daily (bedtime dose most important)
- Cycle: 8-12 weeks on, 4-6 weeks off

### Other Combinations
- **Ipamorelin + GHRP-2:** Viable but redundant (both ghrelin agonists); not an advantage
- **Ipamorelin + Sermorelin:** Similar to CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin; less data available
- **Ipamorelin + Tesamorelin:** Both stimulate GH but via different GHRH pathways; not commonly stacked

## Key Takeaways

- **Selective GHRP:** First and only growth hormone secretagogue with selectivity equivalent to native GHRH; no cortisol or ACTH elevation even at high doses
- **Pulsatile GH release:** Produces physiologic 40-60 minute GH pulse; mimics natural secretion pattern better than sustained-release analogs
- **Short half-life (2 hours):** Requires 2-3 daily injections; higher cost per dose but allows flexible cycling
- **Excellent safety profile:** No serious adverse events in clinical trials; no HPA axis disruption
- **Synergistic with CJC-1295:** Stack leverages complementary GH-stimulating pathways; IGF-I elevation 20-30% higher than individual compounds
- **Clinical development stalled:** Failed Phase II trial for postoperative ileus; remains valuable for research and off-label GH stimulation
- **Moderate evidence level:** Strong in vitro and Phase I/II pharmacokinetic/safety data; limited clinical endpoint efficacy data for body composition or performance
