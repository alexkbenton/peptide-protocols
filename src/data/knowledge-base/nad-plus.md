# NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide)

## Overview
NAD+ is a critical coenzyme involved in energy metabolism, mitochondrial function, DNA repair, and cellular signaling. NAD+ levels decline with age, correlating with metabolic dysfunction and age-related disease. Both direct NAD+ infusion (IV/SubQ) and NAD+ precursors (nicotinamide riboside [NR], nicotinamide mononucleotide [NMN]) have been studied clinically. IV NAD+ remains more established in clinical practice, while precursor-based approaches are gaining traction due to improved tolerability.

## Mechanism of Action

NAD+ serves as:

1. **Electron carrier in energy metabolism:** NADH (reduced form) donates electrons in ATP synthesis; NAD+ (oxidized form) accepts electrons in glycolysis, beta-oxidation, and oxidative phosphorylation
2. **Substrate for sirtuins (SIRT1-7):** Activates longevity pathways and stress responses
3. **Substrate for poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP):** Activates DNA repair and stress responses
4. **Substrate for CD38/CD157:** Regulatory role in inflammation and calcium signaling
5. **Mitochondrial biosignaling:** Central to mitochondrial health, biogenesis, and quality control (mitophagy)

The NAD+/NADH ratio determines cellular redox state; increased NAD+ (higher ratio) supports oxidative metabolism, mitochondrial function, and activation of longevity pathways.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: Randomized Pilot Study—NAD+ IV vs. NR IV in Healthy Adults (2024)
- **Citation:** Randomized, placebo-controlled, pilot clinical study evaluating acute Niagen®+ IV and NAD+ IV in healthy adults. medRxiv preprint, 2024. 10.1101/2024.06.06.24308565
- **Type:** RCT in healthy human volunteers
- **Findings:**
  - 500 mg NAD+ IV and 500 mg NR IV both increased blood NAD+ levels significantly
  - NR IV achieved faster and higher NAD+ elevation than NAD+ IV itself (and in 75% shorter infusion time)
  - NAD+ IV caused more adverse effects (nausea, abdominal cramping, vomiting) vs. NR IV (minor tingling, cramping)
  - Both were safe when infused slowly; tolerability improved with slower infusion rates
- **Relevance:** Establishes IV dosing benchmarks and relative tolerability of precursor vs. direct NAD+ administration.

### Study 2: NAD+ Supplementation in Premature Aging Disorders with DNA Damage
- **Citation:** Promising Results With NAD Supplementation in Rare Diseases With Premature Aging and DNA Damage. PMC12727671, 2024–2025
- **Type:** Clinical case series and mechanistic review
- **Findings:**
  - NAD+ supplementation showed promise in DNA repair-deficient premature aging syndromes
  - Improved mitochondrial biogenesis and DNA repair capacity
  - Reduced markers of mitochondrial dysfunction
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates therapeutic potential in aging and DNA damage conditions.

### Study 3: Nicotinamide Riboside in Long-COVID—Randomized Controlled Trial
- **Citation:** Effects of nicotinamide riboside on NAD+ levels, cognition, and symptom recovery in long-COVID: a randomized controlled trial. The Lancet eClinicalMedicine, 2025. PMC12675013
- **Type:** RCT in 58 long-COVID patients (Boston, 2021–2023)
- **Findings:**
  - 24-week intervention with NR 2000 mg/day vs. placebo
  - Increased blood NAD+ levels
  - Improved cognitive function and long-COVID symptom burden (vs. placebo)
  - Well-tolerated; no serious adverse events
- **Relevance:** Evidence for NR efficacy in post-viral fatigue and neurological symptoms; translates to other conditions with NAD+ depletion.

### Study 4: Nicotinamide Riboside and Skeletal Muscle—Meta-Analysis of RCTs
- **Citation:** The Effect of Nicotinamide Mononucleotide and Riboside on Skeletal Muscle Mass and Function: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. PMC12022230, 2024
- **Type:** Systematic review of 8+ RCTs (total n=342, dose 250–2000 mg/day, duration 14 days–12 weeks)
- **Findings:**
  - NMN associated with small improvements in leg press performance
  - Variable effects on muscle mass (some increase, some decrease)
  - Effects generally modest and clinically insignificant
  - No significant benefit on lipid profiles or glucose control across studies
- **Relevance:** Suggests NAD+ precursors have specific (energy/mitochondrial) benefits but not universal muscle-building effects.

### Study 5: NAD+ and Mitochondrial Function in Aging
- **Citation:** The Role of NAD+ Metabolism and Its Modulation of Mitochondria in Aging and Disease. PMC12177089, 2024
- **Type:** Mechanistic review and translational research
- **Findings:**
  - NAD+ decline correlates with mitochondrial dysfunction, genomic instability, and hallmarks of aging
  - NAD+ pathway activation improves mitochondrial biogenesis, autophagy, and mitophagy
  - Restoring NAD+ delays aging in multiple model organisms
- **Relevance:** Establishes NAD+ as central to aging and mitochondrial health; justifies therapeutic use in aging and age-related disease.

### Study 6: NAD+ Bioavailability and Pharmacokinetics
- **Citation:** Pharmacokinetic studies of NAD+ infusion. Grant et al. and related literature
- **Type:** PK/PD study in healthy adults
- **Findings:**
  - Single 750 mg NAD+ IV infusion (3-hour drip) increased blood NAD+ ~398% above baseline
  - IV NAD+ has ~100% bioavailability (direct bloodstream entry)
  - SubQ NAD+ has high absorption (bypasses GI), but slower than IV
  - Oral NAD+ bioavailability is only 5–15% (poor absorption); precursors (NR, NMN) have better oral bioavailability
- **Relevance:** Justifies IV/SubQ routes for direct NAD+ delivery; explains need for precursor-based oral approaches.

## Dosing Guidelines

### IV NAD+ Administration

**Standard Dosing Protocol:**
- Typical dose: 500–750 mg per infusion
- Beginners: Start with 100–200 mg to assess tolerance
- Advanced protocols: 500+ mg per session
- Infusion time: 2–4 hours (slow drip to minimize side effects)
- Frequency: 1–2 times per week (varies by protocol)
- Cycle: Continuous weekly dosing or 4–12 week blocks with breaks

**Pharmacokinetic Reference:**
- 750 mg IV over 3 hours: Approximately 398% elevation above baseline
- Peak NAD+ sustained for several hours post-infusion
- Return to baseline within 12–24 hours (depending on dose and metabolism)

### SubQ NAD+ Administration

**Standard Dosing Protocol:**
- Starting dose: 50 mg, 3x/week
- Typical maintenance: 100–200 mg, 3x/week or daily
- Titrate based on tolerance
- Peak levels: Lower than IV but more stable throughout week
- Cycle: Continuous or 8–12 week blocks with 2-week breaks

### NAD+ Precursors (NMN & NR)—Oral Administration

**Nicotinamide Riboside (NR):**
- Typical dose: 500–2000 mg/day
- Dosing: Single dose or divided BID (twice daily)
- Duration: Continuous or 8–12 weeks on/2–4 weeks off
- Timing: Morning with food for better absorption

**Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN):**
- Typical dose: 250–1000 mg/day
- Dosing: Once or twice daily with food
- Duration: 12–16 weeks as standard trial duration
- Timing: Morning preferred; some protocols use evening

**Bioavailability Note:**
- Precursor oral bioavailability superior to NAD+ itself (~20–40% for NR/NMN vs. 5–15% for direct NAD+)
- Absorption improved with food (fat, protein) and stable dosing
- Precursors converted to NAD+ intracellularly via salvage pathways

## Safety & Side Effects

### Common Side Effects (IV NAD+)

**During Infusion:**
- Flushing/warmth (niacin-like flush due to infusion rate)
- Nausea or mild GI discomfort
- Headache or mild dizziness
- Muscle cramping or palpitations
- Fatigue

**Severity Note:** Most side effects resolve quickly (during or immediately post-infusion) when infusion rate is slowed.

**Reported Serious Events (Rare):**
- Moderate to severe abdominal cramping, diarrhea, vomiting (in some users, especially if infusion too fast)
- Increased heart rate, chest pressure, throat pain, congestion
- Allergic reactions (rare, but possible)

### Common Side Effects (SubQ NAD+ & Oral Precursors)

- Minor tongue, jaw, or arm tingling
- Mild cramping
- Nausea (less common than IV)
- Fatigue
- Generally better tolerated than IV

### Contraindications

**Absolute Contraindications:**
- Cardiovascular disease (unstable angina, recent MI, uncontrolled hypertension)
- Hypotension (<100 mmHg systolic)
- Severe liver or kidney impairment
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding (insufficient safety data)
- Concurrent monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)

**Relative Contraindications / Cautions:**
- Congestive heart failure or fluid-balance disorders
- Impaired liver or kidney function (reduced metabolism/clearance)
- History of chemotherapy or immunosuppression (may interact with metabolic pathways)
- Medications affecting mitochondrial function (certain antiretrovirals, statins, some antidiabetics)

### Drug Interactions

- **Chemotherapy agents:** NAD+ may interfere with certain drug mechanisms; consult oncologist
- **Antiretrovirals (nucleoside analogs):** Potential interaction with mitochondrial function; monitor
- **Statins & antiretrovirals:** May have additive mitochondrial effects; medical supervision recommended
- **Alcohol:** May interact with NAD+ metabolism; limit concurrent use
- **Levodopa (Parkinson's):** Potential interaction via niacin pathway; medical consultation advised

### Optimization Strategies

- Infuse slowly (2–4 hours) to minimize side effects
- Stay well-hydrated before and after treatment
- Take over several sessions rather than single bolus dose
- Consider precursor-based approach (NR/NMN) if IV tolerability is poor
- Use in regulated medical setting with IV experience

## Synergies & Stacking

NAD+ combines excellently with:

- **5-Amino-1MQ:** NNMT inhibition preserves NAD+ while direct supplementation boosts it; synergistic metabolic enhancement
- **SLU-PP-332:** ERR agonism increases metabolic demand; NAD+ supplementation supports the elevated ATP and NADH requirements
- **Mitochondrial agents (CoQ10, PQQ, carnitine):** Complementary support for mitochondrial biogenesis and energy production
- **Exercise/resistance training:** NAD+ supports mitochondrial adaptation to training; potentiates gains
- **Caloric deficit/intermittent fasting:** NAD+ supports metabolic flexibility and mitochondrial function during low-energy states
- **Sirtuin activators (resveratrol, spermidine):** Synergistic activation of longevity pathways (sirtuins use NAD+ as substrate)

## Key Takeaways

- NAD+ is a fundamental mitochondrial cofactor; levels decline with age and correlate with metabolic dysfunction
- IV NAD+ (500–750 mg) achieves rapid, high blood levels (~400% elevation) but requires slow infusion (2–4 hours) to minimize side effects
- SubQ NAD+ is better tolerated, achieves stable levels, and is suitable for ongoing protocols
- Oral NAD+ precursors (NR 500–2000 mg/day, NMN 250–1000 mg/day) are well-tolerated but have lower bioavailability; established clinical benefits in aging, cognition, and long-COVID
- Clinical evidence: Moderate level—supportive data in aging, DNA repair, cognition, and mitochondrial health, but not universally effective for all conditions
- Most effective when combined with exercise, metabolic agents (5A1MQ, SLU-PP-332), and lifestyle interventions
- Safety profile good in healthy individuals; absolute contraindications in cardiovascular disease, hypotension, and organ impairment
- Synergizes powerfully with other metabolic and mitochondrial compounds
