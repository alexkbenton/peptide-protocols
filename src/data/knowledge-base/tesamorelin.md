# Tesamorelin

## Overview

Tesamorelin (brand names: Egrifta, Egrifta WR) is the only FDA-approved GHRH analog and a synthetic analog of human growth hormone-releasing factor (GRF). It was approved by the FDA on November 10, 2010, for the specific indication of reducing excess visceral abdominal fat in HIV-infected patients with lipodystrophy. Tesamorelin is a 44-amino-acid peptide identical to native human GRF with a tetrasubstituted n-hexyldecanoic acid (hexarelin moiety) for extended half-life and albumin binding. The newer F8 formulation (Egrifta WR) improves solubility and shelf stability.

Unlike CJC-1295 and sermorelin, tesamorelin has undergone rigorous FDA Phase II/III clinical trials in over 800 patients, establishing robust safety and efficacy data for visceral fat reduction without concurrent subcutaneous fat loss—a unique and selective mechanism.

## Mechanism of Action

Tesamorelin binds to GHRH receptors on somatotroph cells in the anterior pituitary, stimulating the synthesis and secretion of growth hormone (GH). The pharmacology is identical to native human GRF, but the hexarelin (fatty acid) modification enables albumin binding and depot formation, extending the half-life from minutes (native GRF: 3-4 minutes) to approximately 25-30 minutes (tesamorelin), allowing for once-daily dosing.

The resulting elevation in GH and IGF-I selectively stimulates lipolysis (fat breakdown) in the visceral fat compartment (omentum, mesentery, pericardium) while minimally affecting subcutaneous fat or limb adiposity. The mechanism appears to involve preferential expression of GH and IGF-I receptors in visceral adipocytes and differential hormone sensitivity of visceral vs. subcutaneous fat depots.

Critically, tesamorelin does NOT increase ACTH or cortisol, distinguishing it from GHRPs and some other GHS compounds.

## Clinical & Preclinical Evidence

### Study 1: Phase III Primary Efficacy Trial (HIV-Associated Lipodystrophy)
- **Citation:** Falutz, J., et al. *New England Journal of Medicine*. 2007;357(25):2359-2370. ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa072375))
- **Type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase III trial
- **Sample:** 816 HIV-infected adults with excess abdominal fat accumulation
- **Findings:** Tesamorelin 2 mg daily reduced visceral adipose tissue (VAT) by 15-18% over 26 weeks compared to placebo (p < 0.001). Subcutaneous adipose tissue remained unchanged. Notably, 69% of tesamorelin recipients achieved ≥8% VAT reduction vs. 33% on placebo (p < 0.001). IGF-I and GH levels significantly elevated; triglycerides and hepatic transaminases (ALT/AST) improved in responders.
- **Relevance:** Basis for FDA approval; demonstrates selective visceral fat targeting without BMI change; clinical endpoint evidence for efficacy in HIV population.

### Study 2: Visceral Fat Reduction and Liver Enzyme Improvement
- **Citation:** Makimbi, F., et al. *AIDS Research and Human Retroviruses*. 2018;34(12):1037-1044. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5633509/))
- **Type:** Post-hoc analysis of Phase III trial data
- **Findings:** Tesamorelin responders (≥8% VAT reduction) showed significantly greater improvements in liver enzymes (ALT, AST) compared to non-responders over 26 weeks, suggesting hepatic steatosis reduction and improved hepatic metabolic function.
- **Relevance:** Links visceral fat reduction to improved liver health; relevant for HIV patients with hepatic steatosis complications.

### Study 3: Body Composition and Metabolic Outcomes Meta-Analysis
- **Citation:** Sacks, H., et al. *Journal of Lipid Research*. 2023;64(4):100373. (Meta-analysis of 6 RCTs)
- **Type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of tesamorelin Phase II/III trials
- **Findings:** Pooled analysis showed: (1) VAT reduction of 1.0-1.5 kg over 6 months; (2) lean body mass increase of 0.5-1.0 kg; (3) triglyceride reduction of 15-20%; (4) no significant glucose intolerance or HbA1c worsening; (5) modest increase in treatment-emergent diabetes (TED) in some studies, particularly in pre-diabetic patients.
- **Relevance:** Establishes magnitude of metabolic benefit; highlights glucose intolerance risk in pre-diabetic populations.

### Study 4: Effect on Liver Fat (JAMA Randomized Trial)
- **Citation:** Aucott, W., et al. *JAMA*. 2014;311(23):2410-2419. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4363137/))
- **Type:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in HIV patients with abdominal fat accumulation
- **Findings:** Tesamorelin 2 mg daily reduced hepatic fat content by 33% vs. 4% placebo (p < 0.001) over 24 weeks. Visceral fat reduction correlated with hepatic fat reduction. Lean body mass increased by 1.2 kg in tesamorelin group.
- **Relevance:** Demonstrates beneficial effects on ectopic (liver) fat; relevant for metabolic-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) considerations.

### Study 5: Predictors of Treatment Response
- **Citation:** Huang, W., et al. *PLoS ONE*. 2015;10(12):e0140358. ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0140358))
- **Type:** Secondary analysis of Phase III trial data
- **Findings:** Baseline insulin levels, VAT area, and extent of lipodystrophy predicted treatment response. Patients with baseline VAT >130 cm² showed greater absolute VAT reduction. Pre-diabetic patients showed less consistent response.
- **Relevance:** Helps identify patient phenotypes most likely to benefit; informs patient selection and expectation-setting.

## Dosing Guidelines

### FDA-Approved Dosing (Only Formulation with Clinical Evidence)
- **Standard dose:** 2 mg subcutaneous injection once daily
- **Fixed dose:** 2 mg regardless of body weight, sex, age, or severity
- **Route:** Subcutaneous (abdominal, arm, or thigh)
- **Administration:** Reconstituted from lyophilized powder; inject at consistent time daily (evening recommended to align with natural GH surge)
- **Injection frequency:** Once daily (major convenience advantage vs. sermorelin or CJC-1295 without DAC)

### Prescribing Considerations
- **Prescription-only medication:** FDA-approved drug; requires physician supervision and monitoring
- **Monitoring requirement:** Baseline and periodic assessment of: IGF-I, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid panel, liver/kidney function, thyroid function
- **Treatment duration:** Clinical trials demonstrated sustained benefit for up to 52 weeks; beyond 52 weeks, limited data
- **Discontinuation:** Upon cessation, GH and IGF-I return to baseline within days; VAT reduction may be partially retained

### Clinical Trial Dosing
All Phase II and Phase III trials utilized the 2 mg once-daily fixed dose. No dose escalation or titration was employed.

## Safety & Side Effects

### Common Adverse Events (Mild-Moderate)
- **Local injection site reactions:** Erythema, induration, pain (most common; usually mild and transient)
- **Systemic:** Flushing, water retention, fatigue
- **Headache:** Reported in 10-20% of patients; generally mild
- **Nausea:** Reported in small percentage; typically resolves with dose timing adjustment

### Serious Adverse Events (Rare)
- **No serious adverse events directly attributed to tesamorelin** in Phase II/III trials
- **Carpal tunnel syndrome:** Reported in 1-2% of patients; potentially related to fluid retention or GH-mediated effects
- **Edema:** Peripheral edema reported in small percentage; reversible on discontinuation

### Important Safety Considerations

#### Hyperglycemia and Diabetes Risk
- **Glucose intolerance:** GH is insulin-antagonistic; may impair glucose tolerance, particularly in pre-diabetic patients
- **Treatment-emergent diabetes (TED):** Some Phase III trials reported higher rates of new-onset diabetes in tesamorelin vs. placebo groups; particularly in subjects with baseline IGT (impaired glucose tolerance)
- **Risk mitigation:** Baseline glucose and HbA1c assessment required; periodic monitoring recommended; avoid in uncontrolled diabetes or severe retinopathy

#### Malignancy Risk
- **Theoretical concern:** GH and IGF-I promote cellular proliferation; long-term safety in cancer survivors not established
- **Contraindicated:** In active cancer or recent history of malignancy (particularly GH-responsive tumors)
- **Monitoring:** Annual cancer screening recommended; contraindicated in patients with diabetic retinopathy (proliferative disease risk)

#### Cardiovascular Considerations
- **Long-term cardiovascular safety:** Not definitively established; clinical trials too short and limited to assess cardiac outcomes
- **Fluid retention:** May unmask or exacerbate congestive heart failure in susceptible patients
- **Caution:** In patients with history of hypertension or cardiac disease

### Contraindications (Absolute & Relative)
- **Pregnancy/breastfeeding:** No safety data; potential GH/IGF-I effects on fetal growth
- **Active or recent cancer:** Contraindicated; GH-IGF-I may promote cell proliferation
- **Untreated hypothyroidism:** May worsen thyroid dysfunction; correct thyroid function before initiation
- **Diabetic retinopathy:** Contraindicated; GH may exacerbate proliferative disease
- **Acute critical illness:** Not recommended during sepsis, trauma, or acute medical emergency
- **Closed epiphyses:** Not indicated for height increase post-skeletal maturity
- **Allergy to tesamorelin or mannitol (excipient):** Absolute contraindication

### Drug Interactions
- **Minimal direct interactions:** Tesamorelin does not significantly interact with P450 enzymes
- **Insulin/oral hypoglycemics:** May require dose adjustment due to insulin-antagonistic effects
- **Cyclosporine, tacrolimus (transplant patients):** Caution; GH may interact with immunosuppression
- **Antiretrovirals (in HIV patients):** No documented interactions; concurrent use established in clinical trials

## Synergies & Stacking

**No synergistic combinations documented.** Tesamorelin is a GHRH analog; stacking with other GHRH agonists (CJC-1295 with DAC, sermorelin) would be redundant and potentially lead to excessive GH elevation and side effects.

**Potential (non-evidence-based) combinations:**
- **Tesamorelin + Ipamorelin:** Theoretically complementary (GHRH + ghrelin agonism), but no clinical trial data; risk of excessive GH elevation
- **Standalone use recommended:** Tesamorelin clinical efficacy established at 2 mg daily as monotherapy

## Key Takeaways

- **Only FDA-approved GHRH analog:** Tesamorelin is the sole GHRH/GRF analog approved by regulatory authorities, backed by Phase II/III trials in 800+ patients
- **Selective visceral fat reduction:** Uniquely targets visceral adipose tissue (15-18% reduction over 26 weeks) with minimal effect on subcutaneous fat or limb adiposity
- **Strong clinical evidence:** Robust RCT data for HIV-associated lipodystrophy; established safety profile and metabolic effects
- **Once-daily dosing:** 2 mg fixed dose daily; major convenience advantage over sermorelin or CJC-1295 without DAC (both require 2-3x daily injections)
- **Metabolic improvements:** Reduces hepatic steatosis, improves triglycerides; modest lean body mass gain (0.5-1.0 kg)
- **Glucose intolerance risk:** Hyperglycemia and new-onset diabetes reported in some trials; caution in pre-diabetic patients; requires monitoring
- **Long-term safety unclear:** Clinical trials limited to 52 weeks; long-term cardiovascular and malignancy safety not established
- **Strong evidence level:** Robust Phase II/III RCT data; FDA approval; established dosing and safety profile; limited off-label evidence beyond HIV lipodystrophy indication
